I have never made a PCB before. That's kinda what made this take so long for me. Thankfully, I was able to get it out in time (And pad out time to get a soldering iron through the program's coins), and I have even learned how to make a PCB! It was a long time in the making (I think like 8-9 hours now!) and I put it off to the last day. I did really good for the most part I would say, pretty closely following the tutorial yet giving a flair of my own to the project. I made a DDR-inspired PCB-Pad, called the MIDI Revolution. It has 4 capacitive touch switches for the respective buttons, 2 rotary encoders for sound and pitch, some mini RGB lights, and of course, the star of the show, the XIAO ESP32-S3. 

  Funny story actually, I had it in the middle with the rotary encoders next to it, but I realized the USB-C port was hard to reach, and therefore impossible to use. I also had some bad wiring and the nature of the project itself made it hard to wire easily. I had to make LOTS of wiring iterations to finally get it right (Mostly at the fault of the SB6812MINI-E lights), and felt like Tony Stark when I figured out that I can just use a 3-way connection to get them wired correctly. I also struggled a bit with an error with PCB edge clearance because of those lights, and it was a quick fix (After about 30 minutes of troubleshooting and a google search). 
  Google was actually a HUGE help here and made the project much easier to do when I was stuck with KiCad. The software itself is really good, I just need more projects to fully pick it up. Overall, this project was a great intro to PCBs for me, and I can't wait to win a soldering iron so I can learn to solder and finish this project.
